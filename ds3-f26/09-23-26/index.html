writing exercise 📝

at the beginning of this project, you came up with an artistic statement describing your collection. let’s revisit that.

revisiting your statement 📄

split into pairs.

choose one person to read their artistic statement out loud.

then, the listener answers the following questions based on the statement alone, without looking at the posters:

  • →
    what is this a collection of?
  • →
    what is the meaning(s) behind this collection?
  • →
    who do you think this collection would be most meaningful to? (who is it for? who is it speaking to?)

now, pin up your posters. answer these questions again based on the posters alone:

  • →
    what is this a collection of?
  • →
    what is the meaning(s) behind this collection?
  • →
    who do you think this collection would be most meaningful to? (who is it for? who is it speaking to?)

work together to create a bulleted list of what’s changed from the original artistic statement and the current poster drafts.

  • →
    what was lost from the original statement that you miss?
  • →
    what was gained from the current poster drafts that you’re excited about?

a note on the public proposals 📣

this is a way of visualizing how you want these posters to exist in the real world.

  • →
    as we’ve talked about, your posters can appear as is, or transformed.
  • →
    if your posters will be transformed, you do not need to actually redesign them! you just need to show us how the transformation will work.
