## 📢 making public

for this project, i’ve asked you to think about your **audience**.

- each of you have compiled collections based on your passions or personal interests. these are collections you may have **emotional attachment** to.
- last semester, you worked on developing a personal voice. now, i’m challenging you to utilize that voice while designing work for **an audience that isn’t just you!**

[slide]

### 🏆 the challenge

take something personally meaningful and make it meaningful to someone else.

- **who are you trying to speak to?**
	- who is your audience? (can’t be you alone!)
	- who *isn’t* your audience?
- **who do you make meaning for your audience?**
	- how does your *organization* make the collection meaningful?
	- how do your *annotations* make the collection meaningful?
- **where can you (physically) reach your audience?**
	- where will you (hypothetically) put your poster up?
	- will you have to transform your poster somehow to make it fit that location? (reduce information, add information, change size, etc.)
- **how can you recontextualize your collection to reveal new meanings?**
	- how do your two posters work in conjunction with each other?
	- are they speaking to two audiences?
	- are they revealing two perspectives?

[slide]

### 💬 let’s discuss

2. what are some examples of audiences you might want to speak to?

## 📌 studio work (analog only!)

work not meeting in a small group, work on the posters using analog techniques with all of the tools you have brought to class: push pins, masking tape, x-acto knives, cutting mats, colored pens or pencils, markers, grease pencils, post-its, tracing paper, etc. work on the posters directly, by hand.

- **develop the curatorial voice** ~ is your collection presented as a guide? an index? a catalogue? a map? a sequence? each would require a different voice and strategy for organizing into an understandable story. 
- **develop the story** ~ consider the organization and placement of the items and explore the use of layers, complexity, and textual and other forms of annotation to tell your story. present information, questions, hierarchies, narratives, captions** ~ use methods that express the nature of your research, the curatorial voice, the values, the connections you’re making, etc.
- **develop as a pair** ~ what kind of relationship is possible between the two posters? together, could they begin to tell one expanded, multi-part story? consider their potential as a diptych.  
- **develop ideas for “making it public”** ~ where, how, and with which public(s) would you like to share your posters? start brainstorming and sketching a mockup and or concept for where and how your posters will hang. just like with annotations, knowing who your specific audience(s) is, will guide you here. who are they? where will they experience the posters? what do they do there? what do they expect to see?
