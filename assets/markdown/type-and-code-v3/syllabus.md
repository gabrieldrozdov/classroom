Type + Code is a studio course in which students combine web programming with variable fonts to create interactive, dynamic typographic experiences. Students will become proficient in code (HTML, CSS, and JavaScript) and type design (Glyphs) to create original fonts, web specimens, digital articles, and more. This course encourages students to push the boundaries on contemporary type design and find new or underutilized use cases for experimental typography, both as a form of expression and in practical applications. These experiments are supported by readings from a wide variety of influences, including video games, performance studies, and computer science.

No prior experience in type design or coding is required.

## 🔍 Overview

This is a process-based studio blending type design and web programming. Here’s what that means:

- **process-based** ~ exploring a wide range of **how things are made** instead of focusing on a single polished output.
- **studio** ~ a class primarily focused on **the act of making things.**
- **type design** ~ the practice of designing **fonts.**
- **web programming** ~ the practice of creating websites using the programming languages **HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.**

:::

[slide]

### 🎯 Goals

Goals are like the **big picture** of what we’ll do in this course.

- We’ll take a hands-on approach toward type design, creating a wide variety of typefaces and web-based typographic experiments.
- We’ll use these studies to analyze the connections between technology, design, and communication.
- We’ll experiment with new technologies to push against the limits of what a typeface or website can be, while drawing inspiration from their respective histories.

[slide]

### ✅ Outcomes

Outcomes are the actual **skills** you’ll leave this course with.

- Workflows and processes for conceptualizing, drafting, analyzing, and refining digital typefaces in a wide variety of styles.
- Dexterity working in Glyphs to compose or modify typefaces.
- Foundational web programming ability using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.
- How to design, code, and launch responsive websites.
- Creative processes for discovering and investigating new ideas merging typography and digital interfaces.

### 💾 Secure Your Work!

This is critical: **back up your work!** Make it automatic or a regular part of your working schedule, to be done every day. You will not be excused for preventable loss of data.

Here are some recommendations:

- ⏰ **Use a cloud storage service** (like Google Drive) to store your files. That way, if something happens to your personal computer, then your files are safely stored online.
- 📁 **Keep everything in a single folder** whenever possible. Sometimes, files require links to other files that can break when you shuffle things around on your computer.
- 🏷️ **Label your files clearly** with the date, your name, class, project, and version when relevant. For example: `240804-Gabriel_Drozdov-Class_Name-Project_Name-v1`

### 👽 AI Policy

::: big

AI-generated work is {h:off-black not} permitted in this course.

:::

why? because this is an entry-level course with a focus on **process, not output.**

if you use ai-assisted coding tools, like chatgpt or claude, you will most likely create more technically impressive projects, **but you will learn far, far less when doing so.**

**if you are suspected of using an ai-assisted tool, you will receive 0 points for the associated assignment.**

> RISD Graphic Design expects generative AI for creative purposes to be used intentionally, and to practice questioning the reliability and value of its outputs. The department acknowledges the nature of generative AI ~ systems which create content by processing existing data, physically stored in massive data centers ~ raises ethical concerns related to inaccuracy of information, bias, data privacy, content ownership, and environmental impact. We also want to distinguish between generative AI use for creative purposes (image and text generation, research, concept development) and accessibility purposes (text-to-speech, organization, language support).
> 
> Every instructor has their own policy for how AI may or may not be used within their class or project. If you are unclear on this policy, ask your instructor before you use AI tools in your work. If AI use is allowed, be sure to abide by RISD’s [Academic Code of Conduct](https://risd.coursedog.com/academic-policies/academic-misconduct), and practice transparency: [clearly disclose and provide citation](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1W_mhMfEeZne4ljOXCvhv2uhG78Z90RpHr-hyeXyQb5Y/edit?usp=sharing) for any AI use in your research or artistic process.
>
> This policy will evolve as technology and our understanding develops, and we welcome ongoing conversation about responsible AI use in our community. We encourage reading through our Considerations when using generative AI, and exploring the Resources for those interested in gaining a deeper understanding of the tools.

### 🚌 RISD Graphic Design ~ BFA Learning Outcomes

The program educates students to become resourceful designers engaged in reflexive, responsible and sustainable practices.

By the end of the BFA program, students will be able to:

- shape typography, image, and content in response to context, audience, and communication objectives.
- apply and develop a range of skills and methods for form-making including visual research, ideation, experimentation, iteration, curation and interpretation.
- design with contemporary and traditional tools, while demonstrating the ability to explore, evaluate and expand the reach of digital and analog technologies.
- create, analyze, and critique visual communication from historical, social, and intersectional points of view.
- develop design methodologies, interests, and forms of expression to generate a unique and cohesive body of work.
- examine and apply visual systems across diverse media and modalities, particularly in print, digital, and 3D environments.
- enter professional practice, independent studio practice and/or entrepreneurship with an understanding of industry requirements, including presentation, communication, and collaboration skills. Students complete the program with a portfolio, ready to enter the field.

[slide]

### 🎓 RISD Graphic Design ~ MFA Learning Outcomes

MFA Graduates are prepared to:

- interrogate the use of contemporary and historical tools, software and theory while contributing innovative and critical formal responses to the field of graphic design.
- produce visual form as proof of concept and demonstration of theory.
- contribute, evaluate and critique visual communication work at an advanced strategic level.
- participate effectively in a professional graphic design studio environment.
- initiate an individual or collaborative studio practice.
- convey their expertise through teaching and/or mentoring.
- curate exhibitions of their work and that of others.
- conduct original research and convey it through appropriate modes of writing, publishing, curating and/or exhibiting.
